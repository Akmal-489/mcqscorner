Ultimate English

1. Noun

Used for giving a name to a Person, Place or Anything.

e.g; Khalid, Karachi, Table

Types of Noun:

1. Proper Noun:

It's a noun that denotes particular person, place or thing. Starts with capital letter.

e.g;

  • Ahmed (Person)
  • Karachi (City)
  • Socialism (Idea)

2. Common Noun:

It's a noun that denotes a general or common person or thing.

e.g;

  • Man (any man)
  • Country (any country)
  • Book (any book)

3. Collective Noun:

A noun that denotes group or collection.

e.g;

  • Army
  • Set
  • Jury

4. Material Noun:

A noun that denotes a matter or substance of whih things are made.

e.g;

  • Water
  • Gold
  • Cotton

5. Abstract Noun:

A noun that denotes some quality, state or action.

e.g;

  • Quality: Cleverness, height, Colour etc
  • State: Poverty, Manhood, Youth etc
  • Action: Laughter, Movement, Revenge etc

2. Pronoun

A word that's used inplace of noun.

e.g; He, She, It, They etc

Note: The noun that's replaced by a pronoun called it's Antecedent.

Kinds of Pronoun:

There are Seven kinds of Pronoun.
  • Personal:
    A word that stands for person.

    e.g; I, we, you, he, she, they etc

  • Relative:
    A word that relates idea to some words.

    e.g; Which, who, whom etc

  • Demonstrative:
    A word that points to things.

    e.g; This, these, that, those etc

  • Interrogative:
    A word that introduces a question.

    e.g; What, who, how etc

  • Reflexive:
    A word that refers to the reflection (turning back) of action upon the action.

    e.g; Myself, yourself, himself etc

  • Distributive:
    A word that refers a person or thing one a time.

    e.g; Each, either, neither

  • Indefinite:
    A word that refers to a person or a thing.

    e.g; All, somebody, nobody

Some Important Points:
  • Who: is used for person; sometimes for long/big animals.

    They never fail who die in a great cause.

    There lived a lion who was very old.

  • Whose: used to show ownership of living/non-living things.

    He is the man whose name we don't know.

    This is the question whose solution isn't known to anybody.

  • Which: used for non-living things.

    The moment which is lost is lost forever.

    The horse which I recently bought is a Turk.

3. Adjective:

It's a word that shows the quality of noun and pronoun or tells more about noun and pronoun.

e.g; This is a book (There is nothing more to tell about the book)

This a good book ('good' is something that tells more about book)

Kinds of Adjective:

There are eight kinds of Adjectives.

1. Proper:

An Adjective that a thing by some Proper noun.

e.g; The English Language. = The language of England

The Egyptian City. = A city of Egypt.

2. Adjective of Quality:

It shows that in what state a thing is.

e.g; A brave boy; A pretty girl; Hot water

3. Adjective of Quantity:

It shows the quantity of something.

e.g; Much, little, some, any etc

4. Adjective of Number:

It shows how many things there are.

e.g; One, two, three etc

5. Demonstrative:

It points towards somthing.

e.g; This, that, those, these

6. Distributive:

It shows the things are taken separatly or inclusively.

e.g; Every, each, either, neither

7. Interrogative:

Used with nouns to ask questions.

e.g; What book is this? (what + book)

Which way will you go? (which + way)

Whose pen is this? (whose + per)

8. Possessive:

Used with nouns to show the possession.

e.g; My, our, your, her, his etc

Comparison of Adjective:

1. Positive Degree:

It shows the simple quality of an object without reference to any other.

e.g; Ahmed is tall boy. ( 'tall' expresses a quality in simple form )

2. Comparative Degree:

It shows a higher degree of the quality, and is used when two objects of the same kind are compared together.

e.g; Ahmed is taller than Shoaib. ( One person compared to another )

3. Superlative Degree:

It shows the highest degree of the quality, and is used when one object compared with all other objects of the same class.

e.g; Ahmed is the tallest boy in the class. ( one compared with all )

FORMATION: Comparative & Superlative

One syllable means (one sound when we pronounce it). e.g; hit, neat, wide

Two syllable means (two sounds when we pronounce it). e.g; happy ('hap', 'p')

Positive Degree Comparative Degree Superlative Degree
(Only one syllable) Ending in 'e'. e.g; wide, fine, cute Add-r. wider, finer, cuter Add-st. widest, finest, cutest
(Only one syllable) With one vowel & ending with consonant. e.g; hot, big, fat Double the consonant & add-er. e.g; hotter, bigger, fatter Double the consonant & add-est. e.g; hottest, biggest, fattest
(Only one syllable) With more than one vowel or ending with more than one consonant. e.g; light, neat, fast Add-er. lighter, neater, faster Add-est. lighest, neatest, fastest
(Two syllables) Ending in Y. e.g; happy, silly, lonely Change 'y' to 'i' then add-er. e.g; happier, sillier, lonelier Change 'y' 'i' then add-est. e.g; happiest, silliest, loneliest
(Two syllables or more) not ending in Y. e.g; modern, interesting, beautiful Use 'more' before the Adjective. e.g; more modern, more interesting, more beautiful Use 'most' before the Adjective. e.g; most modern, most interesting, most beautiful

Use of some important Adjectives:

1. All, Whole:

All denotes numbers, and Whole quantity

He ate all the apples

He drank the whole milk

Note: Whole also means complete or entire

He has read the whole book

2. Many, Much:

Many refers to number; Much to quantity

There are many men in the garden

There is much milk in the jug

3. Any, Some:

Some is used in affirmative; Any in negative and interrogative sentences

He gave some mangoes

He did not give me any money

Have you had any money

4. Fewer, Less:

Fewer denotes a number; Less refers to quantity

No fewer than fifty men were killed in the explosion

We don't sell less than ten litres of milk

5. Later, Latter:

Later refers to time; Latter denotes position

You reached later than your brother

Hashim & Qasim are two brothers, but the latter is more intelligent than the former

6. Farther, Further:

Farther means more distant; Further means additional

Karachi is farther from Lahore than Multan

This task must be done without further delay

7. Elder, Older:

Elder is used for the person, only connection with the same family members; Older is used for both living and non-living things

Aziz is my elder brother

Aziz is older than fatima

This building is older than that
