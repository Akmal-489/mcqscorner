Ultimate English

Preposition of Place/Position

Used to show where something is located.

In - Inside an enclosed space

- The toys are in the box.

- There is a crack in the wall.

- She lives in NewYork

On - On a surface

- The book is on the table.

- The picture is on the wall.

- There's a mark on your shirt.

Under - beneath something

- The cat is under the chair.

- The file is under the pile of books.

Above - at a higher level (not necessarily directly over)

- The painting hangs above the couch.

- Birds fly above the trees.

Over - Directly higher or covering

- A plane flew over the city.

- Put a blanket over the child.



Preposition of Time

Used to indicate when something happens

At - Exact times, festivals, holidays (without 'day')

- The train leaves at 7AM.

- I sleep at midnight.

- We met at Chrismas.

On - Days, dates, special occasions

- She was born on Monday.

- We met on Valentine's Day.

- He arrived on July 5th.

In - Months, years, centuries, longer periods

- We go on vacation in June.

- He was born in 1995.

- It happened in the 19th century.

By - not later than

- Finish this report by Friday.

- Be home by 8PM.

Before - Earlier than a point in time

- Come before 5PM.

- I brush my teeth before sleeping.

After - later than a point in time

- I'll call you after the meeting.

- She left after lunch.

Since - From a specific point in time till now

- I have lived here since 2018.

- They've known each other since childhood.

For - Duration of time

- I waited for two hours.

- They've lived here for ten years.



Preposition of Direction / Movement

Used to describe movement from one place to another.

To - Towards a specific destination

- She is going to the office.

- Hand this note to your teacher.

Into - Entry into a space

- He got into the car.

- The frog jumped into the pond.

Onto - Movement to a surface

- The cat jumped onto the bed.

- Place the vase onto the shelf.

Out of - Exit from a space

- He got out of the room.

- Take the pen out of the drawer.

Off - Movement away from a surface

- He fell of the ladder.

- Please take your feet off the table.

From - Indicating the starting point

- I came from school.

- This gift is from my friend.





Preposition of Manner, Means or Instrument

Used to express the method, instrument or manner.

With - Using an instrument or having a characteristic

- Cut the paper with scissors.

- A man with a beard walked in.

- She painted it with a care.

By - Agent of the action or means of transport

- The poem was written by her.

- We traveled by train.

- He succeeded by working hard.

Like - Similar to

- She dances like a professional.

- He roars lke a loin.

As - In the role of

- He works as a mechanic.

- She was chosen as the leader.



Preposition of Causes, Reason or Purpose

Used to indicate why something happened.

Because of - Due to

- The match was cancelled because of rain.

- He missed the bus because of traffic.

Due to - Caused by

- The delay was due to technical issues.

- Her absence was due to illness.

For - Intended purpose or recipient

- This gift is for you.

- We did if for fun.

- He exercises for health.



Preposition fo Possession

Used to show ownership or association.

Of - Belonging to

- The pages of the book are torn.

- The roof of the house is red.

With - Having something

- A boy with blue eyes.

- A phone with a large screen.

Without - lacking something

- A day without laughter is a day wasted.

- Don't leave without your ID.
